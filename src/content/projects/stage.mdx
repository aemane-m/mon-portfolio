After three years at the <SmartLink href="https://innovation-pedagogique.psl.eu/"> **Centre for Educational Innovation (CIP)** </SmartLink> of **PSL** University as a student assistant, I continued with an **end-of-bachelor's internship (MIAGE)**, still within the **Uses & Tools team**.
It was a chance to lead a more ambitious project on a subject I knew well: **Moodle support**.

Every year this service receives several thousand tickets from the various PSL institutions (*Dauphine, ENS, MINES Paris, ESPCI*, etc.).
Over time, the volume of exchanges made it hard to sort tickets and reuse answers.

The idea was simple: **see how artificial intelligence could help structure this data** and **automate part of the handling**, without removing the human side of support.

<Callout tone="brand">
The project combined workflow automation and language processing to make better use of the Moodle ticket history.
</Callout>

## Technical approach

The project relies on two complementary environments:
**n8n** to prototype workflows, then a **Python pipeline** to industrialise the processing.

<CodeBlock
  codes={[
    {
      code: "python main.py --threads 8 --source crisp_api.json --output anonymized_tickets.json",
      language: "bash",
      label: "Running the Python pipeline"
    }
  ]}
/>

Main tools used:
- **Python** (Pandas, Regex, SpaCy, multi-threading)
- **Ollama** to run language models locally (*Mistral, LLaMA, Granite*)
- **n8n** for task automation
- **Docker** to guarantee reproducibility

<Callout tone="neutral">
The pipeline processed about 9,000 tickets, extracted 1,900 recurring questions and built an anonymised knowledge base.
</Callout>
